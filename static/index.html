Stations are selected by their coordinates within ten miles of the Hopkins reference point (44.925°N, 93.463°W). Names come from source station metadata. Public MADIS airport, Hennepin West, road and neighborhood observations are included when available. Each station contributes its latest report, without rounding its timestamp to a five-minute grid. Source delivery can be delayed.

Measurements failing MADIS QC or physical checks are left out individually. Reports must be within 15 minutes of the newest usable local observation, inclusive; older or unusable stations remain listed with their status. Each usable station has equal weight for each field it supplies. At least three stations must contribute to temperature, dewpoint and wind speed before publishing a new complete average. Otherwise the last complete average and its timestamp remain displayed. Report age relative to the clock is not the coherence rule.

Temperature and dewpoint retain source precision for averaging and display as whole Fahrenheit degrees. Wind speed is an equal-weight scalar mean; wind direction is a speed-weighted circular mean shown using eight compass directions. The observation time is the mean of the actual timestamps of participating stations. Collection checks for new reports every two minutes; most local Hennepin observations are five minutes apart. The Sun updates independently every ten seconds.

Observations: NOAA MADIS. Sun: NOAA calculation details.

About this radar

Native Level II lowest-tilt surveillance reflectivity is collected every minute for KMPX. Completed low-level scans are published from the real-time chunk feed without waiting for the full radar volume; supplemental low-level scans are included when produced. KMPX uses its 0.5° base tilt. Scan frequency is controlled by the radar operator and is not guaranteed. Incomplete sweeps are withheld, and the completed-volume archive fills the previous two hours. Every available completed scan within that rolling two-hour window is included, with no fixed frame-count limit. Super-resolution scans have 250-meter range gates and 0.5° azimuth spacing; the beam footprint widens with distance. Display pixels sample the nearest native gate without smoothing. The beam rises with distance and does not directly measure rainfall at the surface. Times are shown in Central time.

Radar: NOAA/NWS Level II data via NSF Unidata public archive and real-time feed. Map boundaries: Iowa Environmental Mesonet.
