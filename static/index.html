How the average and basic QA work

Current conditions use the official routine METAR for KFCM, KMSP and KMIC closest to :53 within :50–:59. A report at 8:53 p.m. is labeled 9 p.m. SPECI and five-minute reports are excluded. The current average requires valid temperature, dew point and wind from all three stations. Temperature and dew point prefer the METAR T remark in tenths of a degree Celsius, falling back to decoded values when absent; Fahrenheit conversion follows equal averaging with no intermediate rounding. One shared bulk METAR download runs at :00 each hour and supplies the observations and both regional METAR maps. The page arrives with the prepared values and history already included, then reads saved data on Refresh, return to the tab, and after each hourly collection.

The last 12 hourly rows still use routine METARs closest to :53 within :50–:59, labeled with the following hour: 8:53 p.m. becomes 9 p.m. SPECI and rapid reports are excluded from these historical rows. Missing routine reports stay missing. Hourly history uses the same METAR precision and averaging as the current conditions.

A green history dot means basic QA passed and all three stations supplied valid routine temperature, dew point and wind readings. An amber dot means incomplete data or a failed refresh. Hover or focus on a dot for details. Checks reject missing or out-of-range numbers, invalid wind directions, and dew points above temperature (allowing 0.5°C for rounding). Calm and explicitly variable winds are valid. Incomplete historical hours use valid contributors for each field. This does not detect every sensor error or bias.

Wind speed is averaged separately; direction uses speed-weighted circular averaging of known directions. Variable winds have no fixed direction. The sun angle is calculated live. Times use America/Chicago, including daylight saving changes.

About this radar

Native Level II lowest-tilt surveillance reflectivity is collected every minute for KMPX. Completed low-level scans are published from the real-time chunk feed without waiting for the full radar volume; supplemental low-level scans are included when produced. KMPX uses its 0.5° base tilt. Scan frequency is controlled by the radar operator and is not guaranteed. Incomplete sweeps are withheld, and the completed-volume archive fills the previous two hours. Every available completed scan within that rolling two-hour window is included, with no fixed frame-count limit. Super-resolution scans have 250-meter range gates and 0.5° azimuth spacing; the beam footprint widens with distance. Display pixels sample the nearest native gate without smoothing. The beam rises with distance and does not directly measure rainfall at the surface. Times are shown in Central time.

Radar: NOAA/NWS Level II data via NSF Unidata public archive and real-time feed. Map boundaries: Iowa Environmental Mesonet.

Compare METAR clouds with satellite

CLR means an automated station detected no clouds at or below 12,000 ft. It does not prove clouds exist above that level or that they are thin. SKC means the observer reported a clear sky. NSC, NCD and CAVOK have different reporting criteria and are kept distinct. Only reported broken or overcast layers with known heights are plotted in cloud mode.

Cloud bases are above the airport ground level, not sea level, and do not measure cloud thickness or tops. Satellite usually shows the uppermost visible cloud layer, so opaque high clouds can hide lower layers. VV is vertical visibility into an obscured sky, not a measured cloud base. Hourly slider positions use one routine METAR per station per hour, closest to :53 within :50–:59, with SPECI excluded. Both cloud and weather views use only the twelve routine hourly snapshots. Upper Midwest reports are collected once an hour at :00, using the same downloaded reports as the main observations. Late reports or routine reports hidden by a newer SPECI in the latest bulk feed can be missing for that hour; no additional source requests run within the hour. SPECI and five-minute reports are excluded. Source delivery delays remain possible. Each view shows its actual report time when tapped. An 8:53 p.m. report is labeled 9 p.m., just like the main observations. Missing hourly reports are left missing, with no older report carried forward. Some stations do not issue reports within this window and will not appear. Both sliders end at the newest hourly snapshot and go back twelve hourly positions.
