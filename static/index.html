How the average and basic QA work

For KFCM, KMSP and KMIC, use the routine hourly METAR near :53 and label it with the following hour: 8:53 p.m. becomes 9 p.m. Allow :50–:59 for small changes in routine report timing. Exclude special (SPECI) reports and reports after the labeled hour. The newest routine report appears as soon as it is received, so its rounded hour label may be a few minutes ahead of the clock. Missing routine reports are flagged rather than replaced with another kind of observation. Temperature and dew point use equal station weights.

A green dot means basic QA passed and all three stations supplied valid routine temperature, dew point and wind readings. An amber dot means incomplete data or a failed refresh. Hover or focus on a dot for details. Checks reject missing or out-of-range numbers, invalid wind directions, and dew points above temperature (allowing 0.5°C for rounding). Calm and explicitly variable winds are valid. Incomplete hours show a warning and use valid contributors for each field. This does not detect every sensor error or bias.

Wind speed is averaged separately; direction uses speed-weighted circular averaging of known directions. Variable winds have no fixed direction. The sun angle is calculated live. Times use America/Chicago, including daylight saving changes.

About this radar

Native Level II lowest-tilt surveillance reflectivity is collected every minute for KMPX. Completed low-level scans are published from the real-time chunk feed without waiting for the full radar volume; supplemental low-level scans are included when produced. KMPX uses its 0.5° base tilt. Scan frequency is controlled by the radar operator and is not guaranteed. Incomplete sweeps are withheld, and the completed-volume archive fills the previous two hours. Every available completed scan within that rolling two-hour window is included, with no fixed frame-count limit. Super-resolution scans have 250-meter range gates and 0.5° azimuth spacing; the beam footprint widens with distance. Display pixels sample the nearest native gate without smoothing. The beam rises with distance and does not directly measure rainfall at the surface. Times are shown in Central time.

Radar: NOAA/NWS Level II data via NSF Unidata public archive and real-time feed. Map boundaries: Iowa Environmental Mesonet.

Compare METAR clouds with satellite

CLR means an automated station detected no clouds at or below 12,000 ft. It does not prove clouds exist above that level or that they are thin. SKC means the observer reported a clear sky. NSC, NCD and CAVOK have different reporting criteria and are kept distinct. Missing cloud data is shown as unknown.

Cloud bases are above the airport ground level, not sea level, and do not measure cloud thickness or tops. Satellite usually shows the uppermost visible cloud layer, so opaque high clouds can hide lower layers. VV is vertical visibility into an obscured sky, not a measured cloud base. This tab uses the latest METAR or SPECI at each reporting site, including non-:53 reports; the three-station hourly average keeps its existing rules.
