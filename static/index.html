Each airport has equal weight. The newest airport observation is selected across direct NOAA MADIS five-minute ASOS, the IEM archive and NOAA AWC. Text METAR reports contribute their main Celsius temperature/dewpoint group; the optional T remark (tenths of Celsius) and preconverted Fahrenheit values are ignored. The direct ASOS feed is binary data rather than a routine text METAR; its Kelvin temperatures are decoded and normalized to whole Celsius degrees for the same comparison. The Celsius means are converted to Fahrenheit only afterward, then displayed as whole degrees. Wind speed displays whole mph with eight compass directions. Wind speed uses the mean speed; direction uses speed-weighted circular averaging. Variable winds remain variable.

The complete three-station average updates only when all three reports pass basic temperature/dewpoint/wind checks and their timestamps are no more than 15 minutes apart (newest minus oldest). Report age relative to the current clock does not disqualify a station; older reports are accepted when aligned. Otherwise the last good complete average and its original mean observation time are held, with a visible reason. A station is never silently dropped to create a two-station average. The displayed observation time is the arithmetic mean of the three actual UTC timestamps, including across midnight. This is a spatial average of the latest reports, not a smoothed time average.

Background collection checks all three stations every two minutes. Public feed delivery can be delayed even when the station reports every five minutes. Source checks and observation times are shown separately. The Sun updates every ten seconds using full Julian-century NOAA/Meeus equations and standard atmospheric refraction; it uses the current time rather than the METAR time. Sunrise/sunset refer to the upper edge of the solar disc, so the Sun’s centre need not be at 0° at those events.

Airport reports: NOAA direct high-frequency ASOS, Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS/METAR archive and NOAA Aviation Weather Center. Sun: NOAA calculation details · Hopkins reference.

CLR means an automated station detected no clouds at or below 12,000 ft. It does not prove clouds exist above that level or that they are thin. SKC means the observer reported a clear sky. NSC, NCD and CAVOK have different reporting criteria and are kept distinct. Only reported broken or overcast layers with known heights are plotted in cloud mode.

Cloud bases are above the airport ground level, not sea level, and do not measure cloud thickness or tops. Satellite usually shows the uppermost visible cloud layer, so opaque high clouds can hide lower layers. VV is vertical visibility into an obscured sky, not a measured cloud base. Hourly slider positions use one routine METAR per station per hour, closest to :53 within :45–:59, with SPECI excluded. The cloud map uses only the twelve routine hourly snapshots. Upper Midwest airport reports are collected once an hour at :00 from the NOAA archive for the Clouds map. Current uses a separate rapid airport collection. Reports must be timestamped :45–:59 in the preceding hour; the report closest to :53 is selected for each station. Reports that reach NOAA after collection can be missing; no additional source requests run within the hour. SPECI and five-minute reports are excluded. Source delivery delays remain possible. Each view shows its actual report time when tapped. An 8:53 p.m. report is labeled 9 p.m. Missing hourly reports are left missing, with no older report carried forward. Some stations do not issue reports within this window and will not appear. The slider ends at the newest hourly snapshot and goes back twelve hourly positions. Tap a station for all layers and the actual report time.

Below 10 dBZ is hidden. Native radar cells are displayed without smoothing or blending. Colors show reflectivity; echoes can also come from snow, virga, insects, or clutter, and extreme echoes may include hail.

About this radar

Native Level II lowest-tilt surveillance reflectivity is collected every minute for KMPX. Completed low-level scans are published from the real-time chunk feed without waiting for the full radar volume; supplemental low-level scans are included when produced. KMPX uses its 0.5° base tilt. Scan frequency is controlled by the radar operator and is not guaranteed. Incomplete sweeps are withheld, and the completed-volume archive fills the previous two hours. Every available completed scan within that rolling two-hour window is included, with no fixed frame-count limit. Super-resolution scans have 250-meter range gates and 0.5° azimuth spacing; the beam footprint widens with distance. Display pixels sample the nearest native gate without smoothing. The beam rises with distance and does not directly measure rainfall at the surface. Times are shown in Central time.

Radar: NOAA/NWS Level II data via NSF Unidata public archive and real-time feed. Map boundaries: Iowa Environmental Mesonet.
