About the station data and cloud ceilings

Station plots combine openly available UW–Madison Wisconet and Iowa Environmental Mesonet observations from airports, road-weather stations, agricultural sensors, and other reporting networks across the Upper Midwest. Each hourly snapshot accepts reports from :30 through :59 of the preceding hour; each station keeps its actual report time. Click a plot for its name, network and time. Collection runs once an hour at :00. Routine airport METARs are preferred, with special and rapid airport reports omitted. UW–Madison Wisconet five-minute averages supply one hourly sample. Delayed road reports are recovered from the archive into their original hourly snapshots on the next hourly collection. Older readings are never moved into a newer hour. Minnesota road reports may arrive roughly an hour late; the newest snapshot can be incomplete while earlier hours fill in. History builds as collection runs; empty hours stay empty.

Missing values, invalid coordinates, future reports and physically implausible readings are filtered. This feed does not provide complete NOAA MADIS quality flags; local station exposure and equipment can affect readings. Road-weather stations measure air temperature here, not pavement temperature. Source data is provided as-is by its observing networks. Wisconet quality flags are retained; unspecified QC is not treated as verified.

Cloud mode uses airport METARs from the NOAA Aviation Weather Center, collected hourly at :00. It selects the routine report nearest :53 within :30–:59 and labels the following hour. An 8:53 p.m. report is labeled 9 p.m. Missing hourly reports stay missing. Only the lowest known broken or overcast cloud base is plotted; click for exact heights and all layers. CLR means an automated station detected no clouds at or below 12,000 ft, and does not describe higher clouds. Cloud bases are above the airport ground level; satellite shows the uppermost visible layer and can hide lower layers.

Quality checks and station coverage

Temperature and dew point are checked separately against at least four spatially separated neighbor groups within 100 km using a conservative median-based comparison. Minimum disagreement thresholds are 8°F for temperature and 10°F for dew point; locally variable conditions increase the threshold. Two corroborating neighbor groups can retain a real front or local feature. The original station plots retain the per-field quality summaries. Suspect fields are excluded from contours. Readings with too few neighbors are marked unverified; they may still contribute. Screened means locally consistent, not certified correct. A five-kilometer spacing filter limits dense station clusters, and no individual station supplies more than 40% of a grid point’s weight. Winds are combined as vectors. Unsupported areas stay blank instead of being extrapolated.

These experimental maps have no terrain correction or weather-model background. Real fronts and local microclimates can differ from nearby stations. Source flags, physical limits and neighborhood checks reduce bad data, but cannot catch every faulty sensor or a whole group of biased sensors. A model-background check and a monitored station exclusion list are options for stronger quality control.

About this radar

Native Level II lowest-tilt surveillance reflectivity is collected every minute for KMPX. Completed low-level scans are published from the real-time chunk feed without waiting for the full radar volume; supplemental low-level scans are included when produced. KMPX uses its 0.5° base tilt. Scan frequency is controlled by the radar operator and is not guaranteed. Incomplete sweeps are withheld, and the completed-volume archive fills the previous two hours. Every available completed scan within that rolling two-hour window is included, with no fixed frame-count limit. Super-resolution scans have 250-meter range gates and 0.5° azimuth spacing; the beam footprint widens with distance. Display pixels sample the nearest native gate without smoothing. The beam rises with distance and does not directly measure rainfall at the surface. Times are shown in Central time.

Radar: NOAA/NWS Level II data via NSF Unidata public archive and real-time feed. Map boundaries: Iowa Environmental Mesonet.
