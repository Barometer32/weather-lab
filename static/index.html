CLR means an automated station detected no clouds at or below 12,000 ft. It does not prove clouds exist above that level or that they are thin. SKC means the observer reported a clear sky. NSC, NCD and CAVOK have different reporting criteria and are kept distinct. Only reported broken or overcast layers with known heights are plotted in cloud mode.

Cloud bases are above the airport ground level, not sea level, and do not measure cloud thickness or tops. Satellite usually shows the uppermost visible cloud layer, so opaque high clouds can hide lower layers. VV is vertical visibility into an obscured sky, not a measured cloud base. Sources and quality

Airports use the saved routine hourly METARs. Public MADIS adds road, agricultural, fire-weather and other mesonet observations where available. IEM road data and Wisconsin’s Wisconet API fill missing sites; those fallbacks have local checks only. Wisconet’s MADIS IDs prevent counting the same site twice. The source selector and quality selector let you compare the blend. Failed MADIS variables are excluded, and an unflagged copy cannot replace a rejected reading.

MADIS verified means its validity, consistency and spatial checks passed. It is not a guarantee of sensor accuracy. Routine airports are included with local checks in every quality option. Temperature and dew point share the same fixed Fahrenheit color scale. Humidity and wind have their own fixed scales across hours, sources and analysis options. Dew points calculated from temperature and humidity are marked as derived. Wind reflects each station’s exposure and sensor height; it is not standardized to a common height. A finer displayed grid does not add measurements between stations.

About this radar

Native Level II lowest-tilt surveillance reflectivity is collected every minute for KMPX. Completed low-level scans are published from the real-time chunk feed without waiting for the full radar volume; supplemental low-level scans are included when produced. KMPX uses its 0.5° base tilt. Scan frequency is controlled by the radar operator and is not guaranteed. Incomplete sweeps are withheld, and the completed-volume archive fills the previous two hours. Every available completed scan within that rolling two-hour window is included, with no fixed frame-count limit. Super-resolution scans have 250-meter range gates and 0.5° azimuth spacing; the beam footprint widens with distance. Display pixels sample the nearest native gate without smoothing. The beam rises with distance and does not directly measure rainfall at the surface. Times are shown in Central time.

Radar: NOAA/NWS Level II data via NSF Unidata public archive and real-time feed. Map boundaries: Iowa Environmental Mesonet.
